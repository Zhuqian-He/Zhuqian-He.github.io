#### 1. Is Economics More Like Physics or Chemistry? Methodological Reflections on the Semi-Empirical Paradigm
* **Original Title:** 【学术日记】05 | 经济学更像物理还是化学？
* **Link:** [Read Original Article (Chinese)](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/zGIv0tcMAZVXsRZ37kc0Gg)  
* **Themes:** Epistemology, Physics Envy, Semi-Empirical Paradigm, Complex Emergence, Market Design
* **Core Intuition:**  
  Rethinking "physics-envy" in mainstream economics. While physics relies on reductionism and pure deduction from ideal states, real-world economic systems are complex emerging systems with no closed-form solutions. 
  
  Drawing inspiration from chemical engineering (as advocated by Alvin Roth), I reflect on why economics thrives best as a **semi-empirical discipline**: anchoring itself on foundational micro-axioms while leveraging empirical flexibility and institutional context to tackle real-world market misallocations.

#### 2. Why Do High-Human-Capital Women Get Trapped in Low-Quality Relationships? A Resource Misallocation Perspective
* **Original Title:** 为什么高智女性更容易陷入劣质亲密关系？
* **Link:** [Read Original Article (Chinese)](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/kHKQ8B5kxoJCSwjedvlBMw)  
* **Themes:** Human Capital Misallocation, Social Norms, Gendered Incentive Structures, Opportunity Cost, Narrative Economics
* **Core Intuition:**  
  Applying microeconomic intuition to domestic talent misallocation. I examine how highly educated women—equipped with high cognitive bandwidth and problem-solving path dependencies—fall into the "fixer" trap, resulting in extreme opportunity costs, sunk-cost fallacies, and negative ROIs in low-quality relationships.
  
  Furthermore, I analyze how **social norms and cultural narratives** generate asymmetric incentive rules for men and women: while men are socially incentivized to cut losses and protect their market-facing human capital, women are structurally nudged by gendered moral rewards to perform uncompensated emotional labor, leading to persistent misallocation of top-tier talent.
